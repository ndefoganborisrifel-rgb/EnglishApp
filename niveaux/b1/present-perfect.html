Present perfect (simple et continu)

Relier le passé au présent : exprimer une expérience, un résultat actuel ou une action qui dure encore avec have/has + participe passé.

📘 La règle

The present perfect simple (passé composé étendu) is formed with have / has + past participle (participe passé) — I have worked, she has gone. We use it for a past action that has a connection to the present: a life experience, a current result, or an unfinished period of time.

It is different from the past simple (prétérit), which describes a completed action with a finished, dated time reference (I worked yesterday). The present perfect is therefore never used with a specific past time marker such as yesterday or in 2010.

The present perfect continuous (have/has been + V-ing) emphasises the duration or ongoing nature of an action that started in the past and is still happening: I have been learning English for three years.

💡 Bon à savoir

since + starting point (point de départ) — since 2010, since Monday ; for + duration (durée) — for three years, for two hours. Key adverbs: ever, never, just, already, yet.

💬 Exemples

  • I have lived in London for five years. Je vis à Londres depuis cinq ans (j'y vis encore).
  • She has just finished her homework. Elle vient de finir ses devoirs.
  • Have you ever eaten sushi? — No, I have never tried it.
  • They have been waiting for the bus since 8 o'clock.
  • I went to the cinema yesterday.

🔊 been or gone?

He has been to the bank = he went and came back (aller-retour). He has gone to the bank = he went and is still there (il y est encore).

⚠️ Erreurs fréquentes

À éviter

  • ❌ I have seen him yesterday. → ✓ I saw him yesterday. (use past simple with a finished time reference)
  • ❌ I live here since 2010. → ✓ I have lived here since 2010. (present perfect required with since)
  • ❌ since three years → ✓ for three years (for is used for duration, not since)
  • ❌ She has went home. → ✓ She has gone home. (past participle required after has)
  • Don't confuse been (went and came back) and gone (still there).

👨‍🏫 Exercices guidés (en classe)

Work through these together. The answer is given as a model.

  • I (know) her ___ 2018. → I have known her since 2018.
  • They (play) tennis ___ two hours (action en cours). → They have been playing tennis for two hours.
  • Tom isn't here: he (go) to the shop. → He has gone to the shop (il y est encore).
