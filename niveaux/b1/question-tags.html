Les question tags

Ces petites questions ajoutées en fin de phrase (…, isn't it? …, don't you?) pour demander confirmation, comme « n'est-ce pas ? ».

📘 La règle

A question tag is a short question added at the end of a statement to ask for confirmation or agreement (demander confirmation). It is the equivalent of French « n'est-ce pas ? ».

The key rule is reversed polarity (polarité inversée): an affirmative statement → a negative tag; a negative statement → a positive tag. You are French, aren't you? / You aren't tired, are you?

The tag uses the same auxiliary as the main sentence (be, do, have, will, can…) plus the subject pronoun. When there is no auxiliary (présent ou prétérit simple), use do / does / did: You like tea, don't you? She left, didn't she?

💡 Special cases (Cas spéciaux)

I am → aren't I? · imperative (impératif) → will you? · Let's → shall we? · There is → isn't there?

💬 Exemples

  • It's a beautiful day, isn't it? C'est une belle journée, n'est-ce pas ?
  • You don't smoke, do you? Tu ne fumes pas, si ?
  • She has already left, hasn't she?
  • Let's have a break, shall we?
  • Pass me the salt, will you?

🔊 Pas d'auxiliaire ? Utilisez do/does/did

Au présent et au prétérit simples, le verbe principal n'a pas d'auxiliaire : on emprunte do / does / did dans le tag. You work here, don't you? He arrived late, didn't he?

⚠️ Erreurs fréquentes

À éviter

  • Garder la même polarité : You are French, are you? → …, aren't you?
  • I am right, amn't I? → …, aren't I? (forme figée)
  • Oublier do/does/did : You like it, like you? → …, don't you?
  • Let's go, will we? → Let's go, shall we?
  • Mauvais pronom : The dog is cute, isn't the dog? → …, isn't it?

👨‍🏫 Exercices guidés (en classe)

Work through these together — the answer is shown as a model.

  • You are coming, ___? → You are coming, aren't you? (affirmatif → tag négatif)
  • She doesn't like coffee, ___? → She doesn't like coffee, does she? (négatif → tag positif)
  • Let's start, ___? → Let's start, shall we? (cas spécial Let's)
