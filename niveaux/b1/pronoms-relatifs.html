Pronoms relatifs (who, which, that, whose, where)

Relier deux phrases et donner des précisions sur une personne, une chose ou un lieu sans répéter le nom.

📘 La règle

Relative pronouns (pronoms relatifs) introduce a clause that adds information about a noun (the antecedent / l'antécédent). Choose the pronoun based on what it refers to:

who = a person · which = a thing or animal · that = person or thing (everyday style) · whose = possession (dont le/la) · where = a place.

We distinguish defining relative clauses (relatives déterminatives — essential to the meaning, no commas: The man who lives here is nice) from non-defining clauses (relatives explicatives — extra information added, with commas: My brother, who lives in Paris, is a doctor). In non-defining clauses, that cannot be used.

When the relative pronoun is the object of the clause, it can often be omitted (omis): The book (that) I read = The book I read.

💡 Good to know

whose marks possession (possession — dont le/la) : the woman whose car is red (la femme dont la voiture est rouge).

💬 Exemples

  • This is the teacher who helped me. Voici le professeur qui m'a aidé.
  • I bought a phone which has a great camera. J'ai acheté un téléphone qui a un super appareil photo.
  • She's the friend whose brother is a pilot.
  • This is the restaurant where we met.
  • The man I saw was tall. (that omitted — object pronoun)

🔊 Déterminative ou explicative ?

Sans virgule, l'information est essentielle : The students who passed celebrated (seulement ceux qui ont réussi). Avec virgules, l'information est ajoutée : My students, who passed, celebrated (tous ont réussi).

⚠️ Erreurs fréquentes

À éviter

  • The man which lives here. → who / that pour une personne.
  • The town who I visited. → which / that / where pour un lieu.
  • Confondre whose (possession) et who's (= who is).
  • Employer that dans une relative explicative (entre virgules) : utilisez who/which.
  • Doubler le sujet : The book that I read it → The book that I read.

👨‍🏫 Exercices guidés (en classe)

Work through these together — the answer is shown as a model.

  • The woman ___ won the race is French. → The woman who won the race is French (personne).
  • This is the house ___ I grew up. → This is the house where I grew up (lieu).
  • He's the boy ___ father is a doctor. → He's the boy whose father is a doctor (possession).
