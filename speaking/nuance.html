Nuancer ses propos

Express ideas with precision: hedging, register, qualifying statements and sophisticated vocabulary.

📘 La règle

Hedging (nuancer) uses expressions that soften or qualify a statement: to some extent, arguably, it could be said that, there is a tendency to… — these signal academic/professional register.

Qualifying (préciser): add conditions or exceptions with provided that, unless, as long as, except when, in most cases — This works well, provided that everyone cooperates.

Approximation (approximation): broadly speaking, roughly, approximately, more or less, in general terms — more precise than "a little" or "very".

Register (registre): distinguish formal (I would like to enquire…), neutral (I want to know…) and informal (I just wanted to check…). At B2, shifting register appropriately shows fluency (aisance).

💡 Hedging ≠ vague

Hedging doesn't mean being vague (vague) — it means being precise about uncertainty. "It's wrong" → "This appears to be incorrect in most cases" is more professional and accurate.

💬 Dialogue modèle

🗣️ Dialogue modèle — Discussion at B2 level

Topic: Remote working

— To what extent do you think remote working has changed the workplace?

— To a large extent, I'd say. It has arguably transformed how companies think about productivity. Broadly speaking, most employees have reported higher satisfaction, though there are notable exceptions.

— What about collaboration? Some would argue that remote work harms team cohesion.

— That's a valid concern. It could be said that casual, in-person interactions are harder to replicate digitally. However, provided that companies invest in the right tools, remote collaboration can work well.

— More or less as effectively as in-office, you think?

— In many cases, yes. Though I would say it tends to depend heavily on the team's structure and communication habits.

— That's a nuanced view. To put it another way — it works, as long as the culture supports it?

— Exactly. There's no one-size-fits-all answer, I'm afraid.

⚠️ Erreurs fréquentes

À éviter

  • Arguably, it's clearly wrong. → Don't combine arguably (uncertain) with clearly (certain) — contradictory hedging.
  • Unless you don't come, we'll cancel. → Unless you come, we'll cancel. (Unless already contains a negation — no extra "don't")
  • Broadly, the all students passed. → Broadly (speaking), the students passed. (no "all" with a definite noun like this)
  • It tends that people prefer… → It tends to happen that… / People tend to prefer… (tends + to + verb)
  • To put in another words, → To put it another way, / In other words, (not "in another words")

👨‍🏫 Exercices guidés

Work through these guided examples, then try the autonomous exercises below.

  • Soften: "Social media is harmful." → To some extent, social media can be arguably harmful, though it tends to depend on usage.
  • Add a condition: "Remote work is effective." → Remote work is effective, provided that employees have the right tools and environment.
  • Change register (formal → neutral): "I wish to enquire about the vacancy." → I'd like to know more about the job.
