Lecture B2 — The plastic problem

Un article argumentatif sur la pollution plastique. Lisez le texte, apprenez le vocabulaire, puis répondez aux questions de compréhension.

📖 Texte

Plastic was once celebrated as a miracle material. It is cheap, light, strong and easy to shape, so it quickly found its way into almost every part of our lives. Yet the very qualities that make plastic so useful have turned it into one of the greatest environmental threats of our time.

The scale of the problem is hard to imagine. Every year, the world produces around four hundred million tonnes of plastic, and a large share is used only once before being thrown away. Bottles, bags and packaging often end up in rivers and oceans, where they break down into tiny fragments called microplastics. These fragments have now been found in fish, in drinking water, and even in human blood. Because most plastic takes centuries to decompose, the waste we create today will still be here long after we are gone.

Some people argue that recycling is the answer. Unfortunately, less than ten percent of all plastic ever made has actually been recycled. The rest is burned, buried, or simply left in the environment. Recycling alone, therefore, cannot solve a problem of this size.

A more effective approach is to reduce the amount of plastic we produce in the first place. Governments can ban unnecessary single-use items, companies can design products that last longer, and consumers can refuse plastic they do not need. None of these measures is enough on its own, but together they could make a real difference. The plastic problem was created by human choices, and only different human choices will solve it.
